TECHNIQUE GUIDE

Countering

Learn how to read your opponent, bait predictable reactions and turn their attacks into opportunities.

TECHNIQUE UBG GUIDE
01
THE CONCEPT

What is countering?

Countering is the act of turning your opponent's commitment into an opportunity for yourself.

Instead of trying to win every exchange by attacking first, you can allow your opponent to reveal their intention and respond accordingly.

This does not mean simply waiting for attacks. Strong counterplay comes from understanding your opponent's habits and deliberately creating situations where those habits become predictable.

CORE PRINCIPLE Do not ask only "What can I attack?" Ask "What is my opponent trying to do?"
02
THE LOOP

Read → React → Punish

01
READ

Identify the habit

Watch what your opponent repeatedly does when approaching, attacking or defending.

→
02
REACT

Choose your response

Block, dodge, reposition or otherwise respond to the situation instead of panicking.

→
03
PUNISH

Take the opening

Once your opponent has committed, use the opening to regain control of the exchange.

03
BAITING

Force predictable reactions

A bait is an action designed to encourage your opponent to respond in a predictable way.

The important part is that the bait should have a purpose. You are not trying to trick your opponent through random movement. You are testing how they respond.

APPROACH

Make them expect an attack

Approach in a way that suggests you are about to attack, then observe their reaction.

PAUSE

Break the expected rhythm

Not attacking can sometimes be more effective than completing the sequence your opponent expects.

MOVEMENT

Invite a commitment

Position yourself so that your opponent feels encouraged to chase or attack.

PUNISH

Capitalise on the reaction

Once they commit, respond instead of allowing them to dictate the exchange.

04
PATTERNS

Learn your opponent's habits

A

After getting hit

Do they immediately attack back, defend or move away?

B

When pressured

Do they panic, retreat, block or attempt to fight their way out?

C

When approaching

Do they approach aggressively or wait for you to make the first move?

D

After defending

Do they immediately counterattack or continue playing defensively?

05
ADAPTATION

Do not overpredict

A prediction is only useful until your opponent changes their behaviour.

If you successfully counter the same action several times, a good opponent may adapt. Continuing to expect the exact same response can turn your strength into a weakness.

THEY DO ACTION A
→
YOU COUNTER RESPONSE A
→
THEY ADAPT ACTION B
→
YOU ADAPT RESPONSE B
06
COMMON MISTAKES

Bad counterplay

01

Predicting everything

You do not need to predict every action. Sometimes reacting normally is safer.

02

Overcommitting to a read

A prediction can be wrong. Do not sacrifice your entire game plan because you expect one particular action.

03

Repeating the same punish

Once your opponent recognises your response, they can adapt to it.

04

Trying to counter too early

Give yourself enough information before deciding that a pattern actually exists.

07
TRAINING

How to practise countering

01
Watch before reacting

Spend the beginning of a match collecting information about your opponent.

02
Find one habit

Do not try to analyse everything at once. Identify one repeated behaviour.

03
Test your prediction

Look for another situation where the same behaviour appears.

04
Adapt when necessary

If your opponent changes their behaviour, change your response too.
