MOVEMENT GUIDE

Movement Guide

Learn how to use movement intelligently, control distance and position yourself before the next exchange.

MOVEMENT UBG GUIDE
01
FUNDAMENTALS

Movement is part of your offence

Movement is not simply about avoiding attacks. It determines where an exchange happens and who has the better position.

Good movement allows you to create distance, close distance, escape pressure and make your opponent uncomfortable.

The goal is not to move constantly. The goal is to move with a reason.

KEY IDEA If you are moving without knowing why, you may be giving your opponent more information than you are gaining.
02
POSITIONING

Control the distance

YOU OPPONENT
TOO FAR

Nothing connects

Excessive distance can make it difficult to threaten your opponent or capitalise on an opening.

YOU OPPONENT
IDEAL

Threat range

A useful position lets you threaten an attack while still having options to defend or move.

YOU OPPONENT
TOO CLOSE

Limited options

Being too close can make it harder to react and can leave you vulnerable if the exchange turns against you.

03
DASHING

Use your dash with purpose

01

Escape pressure

If your opponent is controlling the exchange, movement can help you reset the situation.

02

Create an opening

Changing your position can force your opponent to adjust before they can attack effectively.

03

Close distance

Movement can help you enter your preferred attacking range instead of approaching predictably.

04

Reposition

After an exchange, changing your position can prevent your opponent from immediately returning pressure.

04
MIND GAMES

Make movement unpredictable

Movement becomes much stronger when your opponent cannot easily predict what you are going to do.

MOVE Change direction

Avoid always moving in the same direction.

STOP Break your rhythm

Sometimes standing still is more useful than constantly moving.

WAIT Let them act

Your opponent may create an opening by trying to chase you.

ATTACK Capitalise

Once movement creates an opportunity, take it instead of endlessly repositioning.

05
COMMON MISTAKES

Movement mistakes

01

Panic dashing

Using movement every time you feel threatened can make your reactions predictable.

02

Moving constantly

Movement should accomplish something. Constant movement can make your gameplay easier to read.

03

Forgetting your opponent

Do not focus so much on your own movement that you stop watching what the opponent is doing.

04

Moving without a follow-up

Think about what your movement is supposed to create: distance, an opening, pressure or a reset.

06
TRAINING

Movement drills

01

No-attack rounds

Spend a match focusing primarily on movement and defence. Watch how your opponent tries to approach you.

02

Controlled approaches

Practise entering your preferred range without immediately throwing an attack.

03

Reset practice

After every exchange, consciously decide whether to continue pressure or return to neutral.

04

Break your habits

Notice whether you always dash the same way, approach from the same distance or react in the same manner.
